## 1. Summary

The core design holds up: store-first alerts, dedupe keys, atomic claims, conditional incident close, a read-only boundary for the Source DB, Zod at every edge, CSRF/RBAC and escaped output. Coverage of the stated acceptance criteria is good. The serious problems are at the **edges of the runtime**: timer overflow, how a single-monitor source error is treated, and the leader start-up and fencing paths. The tests don't reach those paths, because they drive runners and the dispatcher directly and never go through the production wiring in `server.ts`.

Counts: **Critical 0 · High 3 · Medium 10 · Low 13**

## 3. Requirements with no real test (or a test that would not catch a break)

All 13 acceptance criteria have a dedicated suite, and RBAC, CSRF, login and action rate limits, audit (users, recipients, monitors), dispatcher retry and resend, crash points, staleness and timestamp parsing, and templates all have meaningful tests. ## 4. Areas reviewed and found sound

- The Source DB is read-only by construction: frozen service, `_id` + key projection, `maxTimeMS`, validated key and collection, and a static scan for write calls. (Apart from L10.)
- Atomic alert claim (`findOneAndUpdate` with a lock filter), conditional resend (`FAILED` only), dedupe keys for open, reminder, recovered and every-check alerts, store-before-`markAlerted` and store-before-`close` ordering.
- `decideAlert` matches FR-A3 for all three modes, including recovery from a crash between incident open and alert store.
- Auth: bcrypt with a dummy hash to equalise timing, session regenerate on login and destroy on logout, the user reloaded on every request, failed logins counted (400 responses), last-admin guard.
- Error handler: generic messages, no stacks or driver text in responses. Email HTML is escaped and subject line breaks are stripped. The EJS layout uses `<%-` only for `body`.
- NoSQL injection: every id goes through `objectIdSchema`, bodies are `.strict()` Zod, and filters are built from parsed fields.
- Timestamp parsing: ISO strings without a zone are read as UTC, the seconds/milliseconds split is at 1e11, and future values are guarded.

## 5. Open questions for the owner

1. H2: should a per-collection failure (missing collection, query timeout) alert **that monitor's** recipients (per the alert mode), or still go to masters only, but per monitor instead of grouped?
2. H1: what is the intended maximum check interval? (The requirements give a minimum only.)
3. M4: may the app create its own Monitor DB indexes at startup in production (they are idempotent), or should it only verify them and refuse to start?
